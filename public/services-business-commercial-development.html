Business & Commercial Development

Turn a messy commercial problem into a clear development programme.

For owners, founders and directors who know something needs to improve, grow or change — but the problem crosses commercial strategy, product, operations, technology or delivery and does not fit neatly inside one supplier category.

Discuss the opportunity
Who it fits

Useful when the problem is bigger than a single deliverable.

  • A new product, service or commercial opportunity needs to be defined and tested.
  • The business knows AI matters but does not yet have a practical operating plan for it.
  • Several systems, suppliers or workstreams need to move together.
  • A project needs discovery, feasibility or business-case structure before larger commitments are made.
  • Brand or positioning needs to be connected back to the actual commercial proposition.
  • The founder or management team needs an external operator to structure the work and keep decisions moving.
How the work moves

From definition into implementation.

01

Discover

Understand the current position, objective, constraints, systems, dependencies and evidence already available.

02

Define

State the real commercial problem or opportunity, decide what matters now, and separate core work from distractions or unsupported assumptions.

03

Develop

Shape the offer, product, operating model, business case, workflow or project direction and turn it into bounded workstreams.

04

Implement

Coordinate agreed execution, internal people, suppliers, software vendors and specialist inputs.

05

Advise

Review evidence, decisions and open work. Hand over cleanly or continue under a separately agreed advisory scope.

Typical outputs

Clear enough that everyone knows what is actually being built.

  • An engagement brief and agreed scope
  • Current-state and problem definition
  • Prioritised opportunities or recommendations
  • A commercial or development roadmap
  • Defined workstreams, owners and dependencies
  • Specialist or supplier requirements where needed
  • Implementation and decision plans
  • Progress and decision notes during active work
  • Closeout, handover or an agreed ongoing-advisory scope
Practical AI inside the work

AI should improve the business, not become the business plan.

Where AI is relevant, the work can include identifying useful workflows, preparing the knowledge or data needed, selecting suitable tools or models, designing human-review and permission controls, planning or prototyping bounded workflows, and coordinating deeper integrations with client IT, software vendors or specialist developers.

The starting question

Not “Where can we put AI?” but “What business result are we trying to improve, and is AI actually the right tool?”

Boundaries

No invented expertise. No guaranteed outcomes.

America First does not provide legal, tax, accounting, regulated financial, valuation, professional engineering or formal security-certification advice unless an appropriately qualified provider is separately engaged for that work.

The company does not promise revenue growth, cost savings, staff reductions, valuation changes, project success or other results that depend on factors outside the agreed work.

New regulated requirements, sensitive-data classes, major software builds, deep integrations or materially different security requirements move to a fresh scope and specialist review where needed.

How it starts

Start with a short commercial brief.

The initial conversation should establish the organisation, the problem or opportunity, the broad scope and useful timing. Fees, delivery structure and any ongoing advisory arrangement are set after the work and dependencies are understood; there is no standard public price or fixed service-level promise for this work.

Do not send passwords, credentials, sensitive datasets or confidential source material in the first enquiry. If protected information is genuinely needed later, the handling method is agreed as part of the engagement.
